True North Biomedical Engineering Competition

Logistics Commissioner · 2025 – Present

Overview

The True North Biomedical Engineering Competition (TNBC) is a national student competition bringing together biomedical engineering teams from universities across Canada to solve real-world clinical challenges. As Logistics Commissioner, I hold one of the primary leadership positions responsible for making the competition run.

My Role

  • Define competition scope, structure, timelines and specifics from the ground up each cycle
  • Identify and secure sponsors and judges, along with creating clinical test cases for competing teams
  • Coordinate all competition-day logistics including scheduling and team communications
  • Prepare traceability documentation and release files as the primary reference for all teams
  • Maintain weekly communication with participating schools to enforce deadlines
  • Engage universities across Canada to expand participation and grow the competition's reach

What I Learned

Running a national competition pushed me to develop skills my leadership skills, managing stakeholders with competing priorities, communicating clearly under pressure, and making judgment calls when no playbook exists.

Mini C-Arm Project

Biomedical Engineering · Western University

Overview

A C-arm is a medical imaging device used in real-time fluoroscopic imaging during surgical and orthopedic procedures. This project involved designing and building a scaled-down version that captures the core mechanical and functional principles of clinical C-arm equipment.

What I Built

  • Designed the mechanical C-shaped arm structure to achieve the correct imaging geometry
  • Integrated imaging components and ensured alignment between source and detector
  • Worked on system-level integration to produce a functional, cohesive device

What I Learned

This project deepened my understanding of medical imaging physics and the engineering constraints that come with designing devices for clinical environments. It bridged mechanical and biomedical engineering in a way few courses do.

Robotics C++ Arduino

Autonomous Object Sorting Robot

Mechatronics Engineering · Western University

Overview

This project tasked us with building a robot capable of autonomously navigating an arena, identifying colored gems, collecting them, and sorting them into the correct categories, all without human input during the run. I was responsible for both the software logic and hardware design.

How It Works

  • Used infrared sensors to detect and track objects on the arena floor
  • Implemented color-sensing logic to categorize gems before sorting
  • Designed the navigation algorithm to efficiently sweep the arena and avoid collisions
  • Built and iterated on the mechanical sorting mechanism to reliably deposit gems into correct bins
  • Integrated all software and hardware components into a single cohesive system

Result

Achieved 1st place among all mechatronics students, correctly sorting the highest number of colored gems in the timed competition run. The win came down to the reliability of the sorting mechanism and the efficiency of the navigation algorithm under real-world conditions.
